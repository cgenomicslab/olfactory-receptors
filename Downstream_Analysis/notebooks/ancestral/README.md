# Ancestral repertoires

What the reconstructed ancestral olfactory receptors are predicted to bind, and how that
changes down the human tree.

Run the notebooks in order. They share `../../scripts/ancestral_sets.py`, which loads the
matrices and builds the node sets once so the notebooks stay short.

| Notebook | Question |
|---|---|
| `01.node_repertoires` | What do the three core nodes bind, and how do their chemistries differ? |
| `02.pattern_contrasts` | Group ligands by which nodes bind them; contrast the class 1 and class 2 sides. |
| `03.functional_decay` | Does depth in the tree predict how many odorants a receptor binds? |
| `04.parent_child_decay` | How fast does a repertoire change along a branch? Gives the functional half-life. |
| `05.ten_node_lineage` | The same questions across ten nodes, following one lineage down the class 2 trunk. |

Figures are written to `Figures/` as SVG and tables to `Tables/` as CSV. Both are
regenerated by re-running the notebooks.

## About the threshold

**These notebooks call a ligand bound at median probability > 0.5. The extant analysis in
`../reference_tree/` uses 0.915.** That is deliberate, and it is the one thing to know
before reading any number here.

0.915 is calibrated: it is the cut at which the predicted binding proportion matches the
measured proportion over the 23,782 experimentally tested human pairs. It is the right
number for extant receptors.

It does not transfer to reconstructed sequences. Reconstructions score systematically
lower than real sequences, and at 0.915 the three core nodes are left with 5, 9 and 1
ligands — too few to compare. At 0.5 they hold 177, 130 and 113.

So: repertoire **sizes** here are relative, not absolute binding counts. Comparisons are
internal, node against node, where the same cut applies to both sides. `04` checks
explicitly whether the cut is driving its result — sweeping it from 0.3 to 0.8 moves the
correlation by 0.03 and the half-life by 0.018, and the threshold-free weighted Jaccard
shows the same decay. The decay is robust to the cut; the sizes are not.

## Note on the ten nodes

`05` uses nodes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 21, 32 and 44. They are not an arbitrary selection:
1 is the common ancestor, 2 and 3 the two class ancestors, 4 and 5 sit under class 1, and
6 → 12 → 21 → 32 → 44 is a nested chain down the class 2 trunk. That chain is why the
notebook exists — it lets the repertoire be followed step by step along a single lineage
instead of compared across a fork.
